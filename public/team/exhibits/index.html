California Health Care Affordability Project

Welcome! Here's how you'll make your first exhibit.

You'll use AI to dig into real California hospital data and come out with one chart and a couple of sentences that tell people something true. Our model is the UNC Sheps Center, which publishes short, chart-driven briefs on hospital finances. Your job is to rebuild one of their exhibits with California data. No coding needed. AI does the heavy lifting and you steer.

⏱ About 2 hours🤖 3 AI chats📊 1 chart

1Choose what to explore

A good place to start is hospital wages. Pick a job you're curious about:

Registered NursesLicensed Vocational Nurses Aides & OrderliesEnvironmental & Food Service Clerical & AdminTechnicians & Specialists Managers & SupervisorsNurse Practitioners & PAs

Then choose a question to ask about that job:

  • AHow much does pay change from one part of California to another?
  • BAt how many hospitals is the average pay for this job under $25 an hour, California's new health care minimum wage for large systems?
  • CDo nonprofit, for-profit, and public hospitals pay differently? What about big vs. small hospitals?

📎 Sheps exhibit to recreate: Average Salary Expense in Rural and Urban Hospitals, 2018–2022, Table 2 (median salary per employee, by hospital type). For the regional angle, see the companion brief Geographic Variation in Average Salary Expense.

2Grab the data

  1. Visit data.chhs.ca.gov and search for "Hospital Annual Financial Disclosure Report Complete Data Set."
  2. Download the newest year (2024–2025). It's one Excel file, about 12 MB.
  3. Download our data dictionary too. It tells the AI where everything lives in the file.

⬇ Download data dictionary

Peek inside if you like: each row is a hospital, and the first 4 rows explain each column. The dictionary is easy reading too.

3Work with AI

Use an AI that can work with files, like ChatGPT or Claude. Open a fresh chat for each step. For Chats 1 and 2, attach both files: the data and the dictionary.

These prompts are examples, not rules. Start from them, then rewrite them your own way. Keep a few notes on what worked better or worse (a prompt that got a cleaner answer, or one that confused the AI). We'll compare notes as a group.

Chat 1 · The Analyst

Crunches the numbers and makes your chart.

You're a careful data analyst helping me with California hospital data (HCAI). I've attached two files: the data (please use the first tab) and a data dictionary. Please read the dictionary first. It explains how the file is laid out, where each field lives, and the common traps. Use code for all calculations.

I'm recreating this exhibit from the UNC Sheps Center with California data: [name the brief and figure, and paste its link].
My question: [your question in one sentence]

Before calculating, please show me your plan and wait for my OK:
- which fields you'll use (Page / Column / Line)
- which hospitals you'll include and how many (start with "Short-Term General" hospitals, as the dictionary describes)
- how you'll calculate the answer, in plain words

After I say OK, please share a results table, the top 5 and bottom 5 hospitals by name, a chart with a title that states the finding, and a short RESULTS SUMMARY.

💡 When it shows the plan, glance at the hospital count. Around 300 is about right. If it's way off, just ask why.

Chat 2 · The Reviewer

A second opinion: it redoes the main number to catch mistakes.

You're a thoughtful health economist giving a second opinion on someone's analysis of California hospital data (HCAI). I've attached the data (please use the first tab) and a data dictionary. Please read the dictionary first.

Question: [your question]
Their results: [paste the Analyst's RESULTS SUMMARY]

First, please calculate the main number yourself with code, before looking closely at theirs. Then compare. List up to 5 suggestions, marking each "fix first" or "nice to have." If it all looks good, just say so.

💡 If it flags anything to "fix first," take it back to Chat 1 and ask for an update.

Chat 3 · The Writer

Turns your numbers into clear, careful words.

Please help me write up a finding for a short policy brief. Use only the numbers below and don't do any new math. I'd like:
1. a chart title that states the finding
2. 2–3 clear sentences describing it
3. one sentence on the main limitation
Keep the tone plain and careful, and avoid words like "caused" or "proves."

[paste the final RESULTS SUMMARY]

4Give it a quick look yourself

AI is a great helper, but it can slip up while sounding sure of itself. A few minutes of checking makes your work trustworthy:

  • Try one hospital by hand in Excel. Do you get the same number?
  • Read the top 5 and bottom 5 names. Do they look like regular hospitals, and do the numbers seem believable?
  • Compare with an outside source, like a news story, a report, or (for wages) BLS pay for the same job in California, May 2024. In the same ballpark?
  • Match your sentences to the results. Every number should come from the Analyst.

5Share what you made

Send your organizer your chart, your sentences, a line about what you checked, and your prompt notes (what worked better or worse). Put your chart next to the Sheps original. How does California compare? Did you catch the AI making a mistake? We'd love to hear about it!

Questions at any point? Ask an organizer. We're here to help. 🙂
